Research

We design catalytic and functional nanomaterials and engineer them into working devices and processes — from redox flow batteries and battery materials to fuel cells and hydrogen production — and carry them from the lab toward pilot scale.

01

Electrochemical energy storage

Efficient, large-scale storage of intermittent solar and wind power is a critical challenge for bringing renewable energy into today's grid.

  • Vanadium redox flow battery electrodes. Heteroatom-doped and defect-engineered carbon electrodes, the kinetics of vanadium redox reactions on their surfaces, and operando diagnostics of cell voltage losses.
  • Nano-electrofluid flow batteries. Next-generation flow batteries using surface-confined organic redox-active solids to overcome the energy-density limit of vanadium electrolytes.
  • Battery materials. Electrochemically exfoliated graphene as a conductive additive, and Al@Al2O3 core–shell thermal interface fillers for battery packs.
  • Electrolyte chemicals. Fluorine chemistry processes for battery electrolyte materials, including LiPF6 and HF recycling.

02

Catalysis for green energy and chemicals

We design heterogeneous catalysts together with the reactors they run in, from surface chemistry to heat and mass transport.

  • Hydrogen production. Natural gas fuel processing — desulfurization, steam reforming, water–gas shift, preferential CO oxidation and methanation — as well as glycerol steam reforming and bio-hydrogen catalysts.
  • CO2 methanation and other environmentally significant conversions.
  • Core–shell catalysts on metal–ceramic supports with engineered heat transport.
  • Chemical reaction engineering. Compact reactors such as a 1 kW residential PEM fuel cell natural gas fuel processor, H2-selective silica membrane reactors for CO2 reforming of methane, and DFT calculations with microkinetic analysis.

03

Advanced functional materials

We design nanostructured materials whose surfaces, defects and dopants are tuned for a specific function.

  • PEM fuel cell durability. N-doped CeO2 radical scavengers for the membrane, gas diffusion layer and catalyst layer, and conductive carbon black for gas diffusion layers.
  • Metal–ceramic core–shell materials such as Al@Al2O3 (U.S. Patent 12,734,505).
  • Electrochemically exfoliated graphene.
  • Nanostructures by design. Morphology-controlled oxide nanocrystals, metal–organic frameworks and surface-functionalized nanoparticles.
