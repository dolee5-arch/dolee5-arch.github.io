Research

Catalysis in green energy and chemical conversions

Catalysis plays a critical role across energy, chemical and environmental technology. We design, synthesize and characterize catalysts and functional materials, and use them in hydrogen production, electrochemical energy storage and environmentally significant chemical conversions.

01

Electrochemical energy storage

Efficient, large-scale storage of intermittent solar and wind power is a critical challenge for bringing renewable energy into today's energy infrastructure.

We study electrochemical energy storage based on the redox reactions of liquid electrolytes in flow cells — the redox flow battery (RFB). Our current work focuses on high-performance electrodes, the kinetics and mechanisms of electrocatalytic reactions on functionalized carbon electrodes, and the complex phenomena that take place inside working RFBs. Newer directions include semi-solid flow batteries and high-loading electrochemical nanofluids.

02

Catalysis for green energy and chemicals

We design and synthesize heterogeneous catalysts for hydrogen production (steam reforming of methane and glycerol, water–gas shift, selective CO removal), CO2 methanation and other environmentally significant conversions. A distinctive approach of the lab is building metal–ceramic core–shell catalyst architectures that tailor surface chemistry together with mass and heat transport.

03

Advanced functional materials

We design nanostructured oxides and hybrid materials whose surfaces, defects and dopants are tuned for a specific function.

A major focus is heteroatom-doped CeO2 nanocubes and nanoparticles that regeneratively scavenge reactive oxygen radicals, with application to the durability of polymer electrolyte membrane fuel cells. We investigate how atomic dopants such as nitrogen change radical-scavenging kinetics at the atomic and electronic level.

We also engineer the surface chemistry of metal oxide nanoparticles to establish design rules for high-loading electrochemical nanofluids, and develop new synthetic routes to metal–ceramic core–shell microstructures, metal–organic frameworks grown from zero-valent metal substrates, and morphology-controlled oxide nanocrystals.
