Research

Our research has two connected areas: electrochemical energy storage, and catalysis and materials for green energy and chemicals. In both, we tune the surfaces, defects and dopants of a material and follow that chemistry through to the performance of a full cell, reactor or device.

Electrochemical energy storage

Energy storage beyond today's lithium-ion batteries must be safe from fire and explosion, cost-competitive and long-lasting. Flow batteries are emerging as a storage technology that can meet this challenge, and we are developing new approaches to raise their energy density and efficiency.

  • Nano-electrofluids. Design and synthesis of nano-electrofluids carrying redox-active solid materials, and electrochemical analysis of their charge storage, to go beyond the energy-density limit of conventional electrolytes.
  • Redox-mediating chemistry. Applying the redox-mediation concept to increase the energy storage density of flow batteries.
  • Battery electrode design and electrokinetics. Carbon electrodes designed by heteroatom doping and defect engineering, and electrokinetic studies linking electrode surfaces and electrofluids to cell performance.
  • Flow cell reactor design and analysis. Design of flow cells and analysis of their performance, including operando diagnostics of cell voltage losses.

Catalysis and materials for green energy and chemicals

We design heterogeneous and electrochemical catalysts together with the reactors and processes they run in, from surface chemistry to heat and mass transport, and extend the materials we develop to applications such as fuel cells and thermal management.

  • Heterogeneous catalysis for hydrogen production. Catalysts and processes for natural gas fuel processing — desulfurization, steam reforming, water–gas shift and preferential CO oxidation — and glycerol steam reforming.
  • Electrochemical CO2 conversion. Electrocatalysts and electrochemical processes that convert CO2 into useful chemicals.
  • Heat-conductive core–shell catalysts. Metal–ceramic core–shell catalysts such as Al@Al2O3 (U.S. Patent 12,734,505) that remove reaction heat quickly in thermochemical catalytic processes.
  • Advanced chemical reactors and process integration. Membrane reactors, such as H2-selective silica membrane reactors for CO2 reforming of methane, and integrated compact processes such as a 1 kW residential PEM fuel cell natural gas fuel processor.
  • Functional materials and applications. N-doped CeO2 radical scavengers for durable PEM fuel cells, heat-conductive fillers and graphene for thermal interface materials, and metal–organic frameworks for gas separation and reactor applications.
